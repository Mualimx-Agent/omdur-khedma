Legal framework

What actually applies is worth stating plainly:

  • Sudan has no general personal data protection law, and no supervisory authority to complain to. A data protection bill was drafted and never enacted.
  • The Constitutional Document protects privacy, including of correspondence.
  • The Electronic Transactions Act 2007 governs electronic records and consent, which is what makes the consent you give in the app effective.
  • The Law on Combating Cybercrimes 2018 makes unauthorised access to systems and data a criminal offence.

Because the law grants you no detailed rights against whoever holds your data, the rights below — access, correction, deletion, withdrawal of consent — are granted by the operator, not compelled by a statute. They bind the operator through this notice.

What the app stores on your phone

The app keeps a small amount of data on your device only. None of it is sent anywhere and none of it identifies you:

  • the language you chose
  • whether you have seen the introduction screens
  • the providers you saved as favourites
  • the district you last selected
  • the reference number of an application you sent from this device
  • the providers you have already reported, so the same report is not sent twice
  • a copy of the last categories, districts and providers that were loaded, so the app still shows something when you are offline

You can remove the favourites at any time under Account. Uninstalling the app removes everything listed above.

What the app does not do

  • It does not ask you to create an account to browse or to contact a provider.
  • It does not ask for your location.
  • It does not read your contacts.
  • It does not create a permanent device identifier.
  • It contains no advertising and no analytics or tracking components.
  • It does not process payments.
  • It has no in-app chat, so it does not store any messages.

Photos

The app asks for access to a photo only at the moment you choose one while filling in a provider application. It has no standing access to your gallery and does not read photos in the background.

If you apply to be listed as a provider

An application is sent to the server and contains what you typed in the form: your name, your phone number, an optional second number, your trade, your years of experience, your description, the districts you serve, your working hours, your price information, and any photos you attached.

Your phone number is published in the app only if you tick the consent box. Without that consent the application cannot be approved, because the phone number is the point of the listing.

An approved profile is visible to everybody who uses the app.

If you report a profile

A report contains the profile you reported, the reason you chose, and the description you optionally wrote. It does not contain your name or any identifier, because the app has no account for you.

How long data is kept

  • A rejected application is deleted 90 days after the rejection. Until then the applicant can query it and learn why it was refused.
  • A report is deleted 12 months after it is closed.
  • A published profile is kept until the provider asks for it to be removed, or moderation removes it.
  • Photos are deleted with the application or profile they belong to.

If there is a breach

Sudanese law imposes no duty to report a data breach. The operator commits to one anyway: if anyone gains unauthorised access to application or report data, a notice appears in the app and affected providers are told on their own numbers.

Children

The app is not directed at children. Only someone with the legal capacity to contract may apply to be listed.

Your rights

The operator undertakes the following towards you, and answers within 30 days:

  • Access: to know what is held about you.
  • Correction: to have wrong details in your profile put right.
  • Deletion: to have your profile or application removed.
  • Withdrawal of consent: withdrawing consent to publish your phone number means the profile is removed, because the number is what the listing is.

To exercise any of these, contact the operator at the address above and include your reference number if you have one. Customer data needs no request: it never leaves your phone.

Where data is stored

Provider profiles, applications and reports are stored with Supabase, in a data centre in Frankfurt, Germany, inside the European Union.

So your data is held outside Sudan. The hosting provider is subject to EU data protection rules; the operator is in Sudan. Deletion and access requests still go to the operator at the address above.
